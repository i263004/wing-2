Room 9 • Cosmic Titans

Black Holes & Galaxies

From sprawling spiral islands of hundreds of billions of stars to gravitational singularities where spacetime curves infinitely, explore the most extreme structures in the universe.

Exhibit 2.7 • EHT Collaboration (2019)

First Black Hole Image (M87*)

Captured by synchronizing eight radio observatories across Earth into a planet-sized virtual telescope, this landmark image reveals the glowing plasma ring and dark shadow of a 6.5-billion-solar-mass supermassive black hole 55 million light-years away.

Exhibit 2.8 • Messier 31 (2.53M LY)

The Andromeda Galaxy (M31)

Home to roughly one trillion stars, Andromeda is the largest spiral galaxy in our Local Group. Hurtling toward us at 110 km/s, it will merge with the Milky Way in approximately 4.5 billion years to form a giant elliptical galaxy ("Milkomeda").

Exhibit 2.9 • Our Home Galaxy

The Milky Way & Sagittarius A*

Our barred spiral galaxy spans 100,000 light-years and harbors 100–400 billion stars. Anchoring its galactic center is Sagittarius A* (Sgr A*), a supermassive black hole weighing 4 million times the mass of our Sun.
