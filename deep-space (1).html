Deep Space & Cosmic Scale

Beyond the edge of our Solar System stretches an ocean of stars, stellar nurseries, and hundreds of billions of galaxies separated by distances so immense that light itself takes millennia—or eons—to cross them.

Interactive Scale Simulator

The Light-Year Time Machine

A light-year is the distance light travels in one Earth year (~9.46 trillion km). Because light travels at a finite speed (299,792 km/s), looking deeper into space is literally looking back in time.

Exhibit 2.1 • Deep-Sky Survey

The Hubble Deep Field

In December 1995, astronomers pointed the Hubble Space Telescope at an apparently empty patch of sky in Ursa Major the size of a grain of sand held at arm's length. Over 10 consecutive days, it revealed nearly 3,000 previously unseen galaxies stretching back toward the dawn of time.

Exhibit 2.2 • Large-Scale Structure

The Cosmic Web & Filaments

On the grandest scales, galaxies are not scattered randomly. Dark matter's gravity weaves gas and galaxy clusters into a vast, sponge-like tapestry of glowing filaments separated by immense cosmic voids hundreds of millions of light-years wide.

Exhibit 2.3 • Relic Radiation

Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB)

The oldest light in the universe, released just 380,000 years after the Big Bang when the cosmos cooled enough for atoms to form. Microscopic temperature ripples in this afterglow are the gravitational seeds of every galaxy we see today.
