Room 8 • Stellar Nurseries

Stars & Nebulae

Inside towering clouds of interstellar hydrogen and cosmic dust, gravity ignites nuclear fusion—giving birth to blazing new suns and forging the heavy elements that make up planets and life.

Exhibit 2.4 • Eagle Nebula (M16)

Pillars of Creation

Located 6,500 light-years away, these towering columns of cool molecular hydrogen and dust stretch 4 to 5 light-years tall. Intense ultraviolet radiation from nearby newborn stars erodes their tips while dense knots inside collapse into protostars.

Exhibit 2.5 • Messier 42 (1,344 LY)

The Orion Nebula

Visible to the naked eye in Orion's sword, M42 is the closest massive star-forming region to Earth. Astronomers have imaged over 150 glowing protoplanetary disks ("proplyds") encircling infant stars—embryonic solar systems in the making.

Exhibit 2.6 • Supernova Remnant (M1)

The Crab Nebula & Pulsar

The expanding wreckage of a massive star whose cataclysmic supernova explosion was recorded by astronomers in 1054 CE. At its core spins a city-sized neutron star pulsar rotating 30 times every second, sweeping beams of radiation across space.

1. Molecular Cloud & Protostar
